# Marketing research

Grades: **A** = peer-reviewed or large dataset. **B** = practitioner or vendor data, including our own Search Console. **C** = expert opinion.
Most marketing science comes from paid reach and repeat purchase. Request Indexing is free, has no ad budget, and meets an episodic need. Read every A finding with that gap in mind.

## Principles we rely on

**1. Narrow message, broad availability.** (P1, P5; C and B)
Godin: serve the smallest viable audience, defined by worldview. Ehrenberg-Bass: be easy to find for every buyer with a matching cue.
Request Indexing: the message speaks to the owner who wants the reason. The pages answer every indexing cue, including the ones that arrive wanting a shortcut.

**2. The worldview is the destination, not the entry test.** (C, our inference)
Arrivals search for an action and believe indexing is a button. The audience we serve believes Google decides.
Screening arrivals by worldview would turn most of them away at the door. Meet the search, then turn it.

**3. Two-sided messages raise credibility, when the limit is stated plainly.** (A)
- Eisend's meta-analysis: two-sided advertising raises source credibility. Its effect on attitude depends on how much negative information appears, how important the attribute is, where the negative claim sits, and how it relates to the positives. [Eisend 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2005.11.001)
- The integrative framework: disclosing a limit works best on a minor attribute, and when a linked positive follows. [Crowley and Hoyer 1994](https://doi.org/10.1086/209370)
- The Persuasion Knowledge Model: people who see a sales tactic discount the message. A visible refusal to over-promise lowers that suspicion. [Friestad and Wright 1994](https://doi.org/10.1086/209380)
- **Risk.** Indexing is the main attribute for most arrivals, not a minor one. The honest line costs us the guarantee buyer.
- **Rule.** State the limit once per page, after the promise, as a fact about Google. Follow it at once with what we do.
- These studies used consumer advertising. Abstracts were checked; full texts were not. No numeric share of negative claims is quoted.

**4. Expect no excess loyalty.** (P4; A, big-brand)
Double jeopardy: a small brand has fewer users, and they return slightly less often. An episodic need makes return use rarer still.
Request Indexing: growth comes from new arrivals with new cues, not from deeper loyalty. Watched URLs is the one feature that gives a reason to return.

**5. Reduce anxiety first.** (P7; C)
Forces of progress: push, pull, anxiety, habit. Here the anxiety is Google access for a one-person tool, past connect failures, and "will this project last?".
It costs least to move: name the access, show the source, say who builds it.

**6. Position against what people do today.** (P8; C)
Alternatives: Search Console's URL Inspection button, the Page indexing report, a `site:` search, paid submit-only indexers, and waiting.
Request Indexing does not own the reason; Search Console shows it. It owns the reason for the URLs in your sitemaps, kept over time.

**7. Search is a proven door with a low ceiling.** (B, our data)
- Clicks stayed flat for 16 months. The name brings at least 37% of them.
- US demand for the head term is about 140 searches a month. Diagnosis terms are 10 to 30 each, and "crawled not indexed" fell from 590 in early 2025.
- Most clicks come from outside the US, where volume is unmeasured here.
- Growth needs more cues: Search Console's button, the reason pages, and the index checker.

**8. Agents are gscdump's shelf.** (P10; A benchmark, C field)
Tool descriptions and directory text drive agent selection. No Request Indexing account in the last 90 days holds an active API key, and almost none of gscdump's MCP accounts came through it.
Request Indexing links to gscdump once and spends no marketing effort on agent surfaces.

## Advisor reviews

Three independent Fable advisors read the same evidence brief on 2026-11-17. Confidence is each advisor's own.

| Lens | Audience pick | H1 | Key warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Godin | The site owner who pressed the button, saw nothing, and wants the truth more than a trick (65) | "Requested indexing? See what Google decided." | "Indie developer" describes the founder's followers, not the search arrivals |
| Evidence and science | Owners of 1 to 10 Sites who already pressed Google's button, mostly outside the US (65) | Keep the action words; refuse the guarantee in the promise, never at the search door | The strongest cues are the ones the product cannot honestly serve |
| Contrarian | The non-technical owner or freelancer with a new or small site (60) | "Request indexing. Then see what Google decided." | Agent surfaces contradict "Not for agent operators"; the boundary with gscdump is the interface, not Site count |

**Where they agree:** the current "indie developer" audience is unsupported; the H1 breaks the file's own promise rule; agent surfaces do not belong in Request Indexing marketing. The advisors had no funnel data. The funnel read afterwards supports them: 9 in 10 signups use a consumer email, most have one Site, and no account holds an API key.
**Where they differ:** how much of the head term the H1 keeps. `docs/marketing/home-page.md` sets a rank guard for that risk.

## Gaps

- Return use, and whether the first evidence view records.
- Signup source covers about 5% of signups.
- No role data. Consumer email addresses, single Sites, and the country mix suggest individual owners; nothing measures it.
- About 20 customer messages across two years.
- US-only keyword volumes.
